# How consulting teams structure problems, and the tools and AI they use and allow (state as of 2026-11-26)

Scope: MBB (McKinsey, BCG, Bain), the Big Four (Deloitte, PwC, EY, KPMG), Accenture, and boutique firms (5–200 people). International focus. How reliable each item is gets marked inline: **[primary]** means a firm or vendor press release or the firm's own site; **[press]** means a news outlet; **[secondary]** means a blog, aggregator or estimate; **[vendor claim]** means marketing by an interested party; **[forum/opinion]**. Older items carry their date. Research method: roughly 45 web searches and fetches on 2026-11-26. Several mckinsey.com pages returned HTTP 503, so I cite McKinsey items through press coverage where I had to.

## 1. How consulting teams structure problems in practice, and which published sources describe it

### Takeaway
The core practice is stable and well documented. Teams form an early "answer" (the hypothesis, or the "one-day answer"), break the problem into a logic, issue or hypothesis tree that aims to be MECE, prune it to the branches that matter, and test each branch with data. They storyline early as a ghost deck, a skeleton of action titles, and they communicate with the answer first (Pyramid Principle). The trees "all started on a sketchpad or a whiteboard" (Conn & McLean). AI has entered this loop: McKinsey and Bain now test candidates with AI tools. But the thinking step (structuring, choosing which hypotheses to kill, deciding) is still done by people and is where firms say their value lies.

### Cited Findings
**Primary and book sources**
- *Bulletproof Problem Solving* (Charles Conn and Robert McLean, both ex-McKinsey; Wiley, 2018) gives a seven-step cycle: define, disaggregate, prioritize (prune the tree), workplan, analyze, synthesize, communicate. It is described as "both a complete process and an iterative cycle" that "can be completed over any timeframe with the information at hand". — [Wiley chapter 1 excerpt (PDF)](https://catalogimages.wiley.com/images/db/pdf/9781119553021.excerpt.pdf); step names also in [Leading Blog summary, Oct 2019](https://www.leadershipnow.com/leadingblog/2019/10/7_steps_to_bulletproof_problem.html) and McKinsey's own article ["How to master the seven-step problem-solving process"](https://www.mckinsey.com/capabilities/strategy-and-corporate-finance/our-insights/how-to-master-the-seven-step-problem-solving-process) (the page exists; it returned 503 when I fetched it)
- "One-day answer" in the authors' words: "We often use the expression, 'What's the one-day answer?' This means we ask our team to have a coherent summary of our best understanding of the problem and a solution path at any point in the project, not just at the end. This process of creating active hypotheses is at the heart of Bulletproof Problem Solving." — [Wiley excerpt](https://catalogimages.wiley.com/images/db/pdf/9781119553021.excerpt.pdf) [primary/book]
- Logic trees, in the same book: "We use logic or issue trees to visualize and disaggregate problems. We employ several types, including hypothesis trees and decision trees… We learned the power of logic trees at McKinsey". Trees "lead to clear hypotheses that can be tested with data and analysis… they all started on a sketchpad or a whiteboard." The authors also say solo problem solvers should build in review "to get the higher objectivity and other bias-fighting benefits of a team." — [Wiley excerpt](https://catalogimages.wiley.com/images/db/pdf/9781119553021.excerpt.pdf)
- In McKinsey interviews (Rob McLean's account), candidates were "encouraged… to use a whiteboard or pad of paper", and getting the breakdown right "is usually a trial and error process". — [Wiley excerpt](https://catalogimages.wiley.com/images/db/pdf/9781119553021.excerpt.pdf)
- Ghost deck: an early draft deck, "also referred to as a shell or skeleton at other firms". It is built to test the storyline before heavy analysis because "if a storyline has to be changed, it's much better to know sooner", and "often, what is most apparent from the ghost deck is what is missing." — [Working With McKinsey blog, July 2013](https://workingwithmckinsey.blogspot.com/2013/07/McKinsey-presentations-ghost-decks.html) [anonymous ex-insider blog, 2013]
- Storyline pressure test: read only the slide titles, which "should form a coherent narrative". — [Umbrex, Storylining and Deck Structure](https://umbrex.com/resources/the-consulting-career-guide/storylining-and-deck-structure/) [secondary; Umbrex is a network of independent ex-MBB consultants]
- Management Consulted runs Pyramid Principle training, which shows it is still a core taught skill. Its COO Namaan Mian: "Stakeholders don't necessarily want more data; they want more confidence." — [Management Consulted, Pyramid Principle training](https://managementconsulted.com/pyramid-principle-training/); [Management Consulted, "Why Great Consulting Starts With Great Storytelling"](https://managementconsulted.com/media/why-great-consulting-starts-with-great-storytelling/)

**Case-prep and training sources (secondary)**
- Hypothesis-driven approach: "Consultants formulate a hypothesis for the solution to a business problem, then gather data to support or disprove it." MECE means "no overlaps and no gaps". A common mistake is skipping levels and assuming the hypothesis is true instead of confirming it. Hypotheses "should change when new evidence changes your understanding". — [MyConsultingOffer, hypothesis tree](https://www.myconsultingoffer.org/case-study-interview-prep/hypothesis-tree); [MyConsultingOffer, hypothesis-driven approach](https://www.myconsultingoffer.org/case-study-interview-prep/hypothesis-driven-approach/); [CaseInterview.com](https://caseinterview.com/hypothesis-in-case-interview)
- Sources disagree on terminology. One treats hypothesis trees (testing a specific hypothesis) and issue trees (listing all potential issues) as different. Another says the two terms are used interchangeably in practice. — [MyConsultingOffer](https://www.myconsultingoffer.org/case-study-interview-prep/hypothesis-tree); [Umbrex, Building Better Issue Trees](https://umbrex.com/resources/the-future-consultants-guide-to-case-interviews/building-better-issue-trees/); [PrepLounge issue tree](https://www.preplounge.com/en/case-interview-basics/issue-tree)
- A typical taught sequence: problem definition, structuring, hypothesis generation, storyline development, visual presentation. — [Business Consultancy Society workshop deck (SlideShare)](https://www.slideshare.net/BusinessConsultancySociety/bcs-problemsolving-presentation-workshop) [student society]
- Whiteboards in client meetings work as brainstorm space and as the "meeting minutes" (a whiteboard equals meeting minutes), with ideas grouped into buckets to structure the problem. — [Consultant's Mind blog](https://consultantsmind.com/?p=6244) [practitioner blog]
- Team problem-solving sessions are a recurring ritual. A "day in the life" piece describes an analyst preparing a slide for "the afternoon's problem-solving session" from the previous night's analysis. — [St John's / Leland, Dec 16 2025](https://careerservices.stjohns.edu/blog/2025/12/16/a-day-in-the-life-of-a-mckinsey-management-consultant-leland/) [secondary]
- On the purpose of a kickoff: "If people leave with different understandings of the problem, scope, or how you'll work together, the kickoff failed." — [skills.sh management-consulting-plugin, engagement-setup](https://skills.sh/anotb/management-consulting-plugin/engagement-setup) [community AI-agent skill]

**AI entering the structuring step**
- McKinsey asks some graduate candidates "to work with an internal tool called Lilli to complete applied exercises resembling real client scenarios" in final rounds (Jan 2026). — [HR Grapevine, 2026-01-15](https://www.hrgrapevine.com/us/content/article/2026-01-15-mckinsey-goes-all-in-on-ai-with-interview-testing-workforce-of-20000-agents) [press]
- Bain also runs an AI component in interviews (2026). — [CaseBasix, Bain AI interview](https://www.casebasix.com/pages/bain-ai-interview) [secondary]
- Opinion: once anyone can generate a MECE breakdown, the differentiator moves away from whether you decomposed the problem. — [Noel D'Costa, "Structured Thinking: The Consultant's Edge in 2026"](https://noeldcosta.com/structured-thinking-problem-solving/) [opinion]
- Vendor warning: "a tree that looks MECE is not necessarily MECE". — [Mindomo issue trees](https://www.mindomo.com/use-cases/idea-organization/issue-trees/) [vendor claim]

### Inferences
- Vellum's model maps closely onto the documented method. Lettered directions match competing hypotheses. "A test it could fail" matches hypothesis testing ("support or disprove"). The trail of rejected options matches pruning the tree. Wright's page or deck matches the ghost deck. Critic matches the "bias-fighting benefits of a team" that Conn & McLean prescribe. Firms already use this vocabulary, so the pitch can be written in consultant terms (one-day answer, ghost deck, kill the hypothesis) rather than generic AI terms.
- The documented gap is continuity. The one-day answer must be current "at any point in the project", yet whiteboard trees are ephemeral, which is why the whiteboard doubles as minutes. A persistent map that keeps the current answer, the tree, the evidence and the killed branches answers that need directly.
- Interviews testing candidates with Lilli suggests firms see AI-assisted structuring as a core skill. A tool that trains or shows structured thinking could also sell to case-prep and training markets, a possible side door.

### Gaps
- I found no primary firm document (McKinsey, BCG or Bain training material) describing kickoff whiteboarding or the "team problem-solving session" in detail. The descriptions come from books, alumni blogs and prep sites.
- Canonical texts not fetched this session (bibliographic details are general knowledge, not checked here): Ethan Rasiel, *The McKinsey Way* (1999); Rasiel & Friga, *The McKinsey Mind* (2001); Barbara Minto, *The Pyramid Principle* (Minto was a McKinsey alumna; the book is decades old). A report writer should mark these as standard references, not verified claims.
- I found no data on how much kickoff or meeting time teams spend whiteboarding. No survey quantifies it.

## 2. Which tools consulting teams use for this today, and the pains of going from whiteboard to slides

### Takeaway
The working stack is still physical whiteboard or paper, then PowerPoint (with think-cell for charts and firm-internal toolbars) and Excel, with Miro or Mural for remote workshops. In 2025–26 an AI layer was added on top: firm chatbots, Copilot, Claude or ChatGPT for storylining, and AI slide drafting into firm templates. The whiteboard-to-slides handoff is still manual. Miro has no native editable PowerPoint export (per a vendor comparison), conversion is done by rebuilding, and several 2026 AI slide vendors now market "whiteboard photo to native slide" as a feature. That is indirect evidence of the pain.

### Cited Findings
**PowerPoint, add-ins, internal toolbars**
- MBB firms run proprietary PowerPoint tooling that cannot be bought: McKinsey "Marvin Tools", BCG internal macros (originally "Cool macros"), the "Bain Toolbox". UpSlide is "used by firms like KPMG, Rothschild, and Apollo". Published add-in prices (Aug 2025): Macabacus $200+/user/yr; Efficient Elements €99/user/yr; PPT Productivity $179/yr; empower Express €29.99/mo. — [Deckary, "8 Best PowerPoint Add-ins for Consultants", 2025-08-25](https://deckary.com/blog/what-powerpoint-add-ins-do-consultants-use) [vendor; the author says he is ex-McKinsey/Deloitte]
- think-cell is still the charting standard. It released **think-cell Assist** (AI) for PowerPoint and Excel in June 2026. — [Wikipedia, think-cell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Think-cell) [secondary]. As of Oct 2026, think-cell Assist is in early access as part of "AI Suite" packages, with per-user tiers sold through sales (no public prices), an ISO 27001 badge, a DPA saying input data isn't used for training, and processing in Berlin. — [NOXI, "Best AI Presentation Tools for Consultants", 2026-10-11](https://noxi.ai/blog/best-ai-presentation-tools-for-consultants) [competing vendor]
- A 2026 vendor ranking describes consultants splitting the work three ways: storylining (ChatGPT or Claude), drafting slides into the firm template (Copilot in PowerPoint, Plus AI and similar), and exhibits (think-cell). — [NOXI, Oct 2026](https://noxi.ai/blog/best-ai-presentation-tools-for-consultants) [vendor claim]
- Microsoft 365 Copilot is the default where firms already license M365: $30/user/month (annual, enterprise) on top of an M365 licence; the business add-on is $21/user/mo. Brand kit from an uploaded .potx. — [Oria, updated 2026-09-20](https://www.oria.one/resources/best-ai-presentation-tool-strategy-consultants); [NOXI, Oct 2026](https://noxi.ai/blog/best-ai-presentation-tools-for-consultants) [vendors]
- In one vendor's enterprise test, Claude for PowerPoint produced "zero action titles". — [Perceptis, "Best Enterprise PowerPoint AI Add-ins in 2026"](https://perceptis.ai/blog/enterprise-powerpoint-ai-add-ins-2026) [competing vendor; a single test]
- McKinsey runs a "Tone of Voice" agent that checks reports against house writing style, a task juniors previously did by hand (May 2025). — [Business Insider Japan, May 2025](https://www.businessinsider.jp/article/2505-consulting-ai-mckinsey-bcg-deloitte-pwc-kpmg-chatbots-ai-tools/) [press]
- Headlines from 2025: "McKinsey Is Using AI to Create PowerPoints and Take Over Junior Employee Tasks" — [Entrepreneur](https://www.entrepreneur.com/business-news/ai-creates-powerpoints-at-mckinsey-replacing-junior-workers/492624); Fortune on McKinsey AI for PowerPoints and proposals, 2025-06-02 — [Fortune](https://dc.fortune.com/2025/06/02/mckinsey-ai-consulting-powerpoints-proposal-technology) [press; headline level only, not fetched]

**Whiteboards and canvas tools**
- Miro markets a "frame-to-slide workflow [that] turns the canvas directly into a structured deck" for strategy consultants, which means presenting from the board rather than rebuilding in PowerPoint. — [Miro, slides for strategy consultants](https://miro.com/presentations/slides-strategy-consultants/) [vendor claim]
- Mural "suits facilitators, consultants and larger organisations that run repeatable workshops and want frameworks to follow." — [Lilach Bullock, Miro vs Mural](https://www.lilachbullock.com/miro-vs-mural/) [secondary]
- Miro exports boards only as JPG, PDF or CSV, with "no native PowerPoint export option", so exports are effectively screenshots. — reported in a vendor comparison of Miro alternatives that sells a conversion product ([Presentations.ai, Miro alternatives](https://www.presentations.ai/blog/miro-alternatives)) [vendor claim; I attribute it to this page from the search summary, not from a full fetch]
- A conversion services firm describes rebuilding a client's Miro boards as editable PowerPoint, frame by frame, with a master slide structure. — [Helion360](https://helion360.com/blog/miro-boards-to-powerpoint-presentations) [vendor anecdote]
- Oria (2026) markets turning "outlines, sketches, whiteboard photos, or busy slides into native, editable slides" in PowerPoint or through Claude or ChatGPT, at $19/mo (60 tokens), $59 (200), or $399 (shared 1,200). The page cites Nancy Duarte's estimate of 30–90 hours of preparation per hour presented. — [Oria, Jul 2026, updated Sep 2026](https://www.oria.one/resources/best-ai-presentation-tool-strategy-consultants) [vendor claim]
- Tome (AI storytelling decks) was sunset in March 2025. — [Oria](https://www.oria.one/resources/best-ai-presentation-tool-strategy-consultants) [secondary]

### Inferences
- The pain is in two places. (a) The thinking captured on a whiteboard or in Miro is lost or re-typed when it becomes a deck. (b) The deck flattens the reasoning: rejected branches, evidence weight and why an option was chosen disappear. Vendors attack (a) with photo-to-slide and frame-to-slide features. Nobody found here addresses (b). Vellum's trail of decisions and rejected options is the unclaimed part.
- Anything Wright produces must land as native, editable PowerPoint in the firm's own template, with action titles and think-cell-compatible charts. That is the format consultants are judged on. HTML pages or web decks will be seen as drafts at best. Vendor tests already rank tools on action titles and overflow handling.
- Inside MBB, slide drafting is already covered by internal tools (Deckster, Marvin, Toolbox) plus Copilot. Wright's deck output will not differentiate there. The reasoning map and trail are the differentiator.

### Gaps
- I found no reliable data on Miro, Mural or FigJam penetration at named consulting firms, or on whether firms restrict them for client data.
- I found no r/consulting threads describing whiteboard-to-slides pain. Searches returned no Reddit content (the search tool doesn't index it well), so practitioner sentiment here is unverified.
- The Teams and Zoom whiteboard share in consulting is not covered by any source I found.

## 3. Internal AI tools at large firms, how widely they are used, and the rules on outside AI tools with client data

### Takeaway
By late 2026 every large firm has (a) a firm-wide frontier-model deployment inside its own enterprise tenancy: Claude at Deloitte (≈470k), KPMG (276k+), Accenture and PwC (partly); ChatGPT Enterprise at Bain (all staff), PwC (100k, 2024) and Accenture; Copilot widely. Most also have (b) a proprietary knowledge or slide layer: McKinsey Lilli, BCG Deckster and GENE, Bain Sage. Usage claims are high (70–90% of staff), but almost all are self-reported. The rule on outside tools is consistent: client or firm data goes only into approved enterprise tools. Unapproved and consumer tools are barred, new tools go through review, and some client contracts require written client approval of any AI tool. Clients are wary: a 2026 Source Global Research poll found 70% would not trust a report made with AI.

Cyberhaven found sensitive data in 39.7% of data movements into AI tools. — [aona.ai roundup](https://aona.ai/blog/shadow-ai-statistics-2026/); [skilder roundup](https://www.skilder.ai/en/blog/shadow-ai-statistics-2026/); [Cybersecurity Dive on Okta survey](https://www.cybersecuritydive.com/news/shadow-ai-enterprise-data-policies-okta/821344/) [secondary roundups]

Almost a third said learning that a firm used AI in a published report would shake their confidence. 77% of clients who had used consultants' AI tools think AI is a bubble, vs 55% who had not. Fiona Czerniawska: "exposure ought to build confidence." — [Resultsense 2026-08-04, citing The Telegraph](https://www.resultsense.com/news/2026-08-04-consultancy-clients-distrust-ai-reports/) [press, second-hand]
- Thomson Reuters 2026 professional services research: organization-wide AI use at 40%, up from 22% in 2025 (1,500+ respondents, 27 countries). — [Tommaso Maria Ricci summary](https://www.tommasomariaricci.com/blog/ai-for-professional-services-guide) [secondary]

### Inferences
- In large firms the AI seat is taken. Each runs a frontier model inside its own tenancy with negotiated data terms. An outside tool that brings its own cloud AI faces the "unapproved tool" barrier by default. The realistic paths: (1) Vellum runs fully local, or calls the firm's already-approved model endpoint (Azure OpenAI, the firm's Claude tenancy, Bedrock or Vertex) with the firm's keys, so no new data processor is added; or (2) Vellum is approved through a pilot inside a practice or a lab group. Local-first is a real advantage here, not just a privacy slogan.
- The Lilli breach (Mar 2026) and the Deloitte Australia refund (Oct 2025) will make security and AI-quality reviewers stricter in 2026–27. A tool that keeps source provenance and shows which claims came from which evidence, and what a human decided, answers the "hallucinated citations" failure directly.
- The Source Global Research result (70% distrust AI-made reports) suggests firms need to show that humans did the judging. Vellum's decision trail, evidence weights, and "the person decides" model can be positioned as client-facing proof of human judgment, an exportable audit trail, not just an internal workspace.
- Firm tools focus on knowledge retrieval (Lilli, Sage), slides (Deckster) and agents for routine work. None of the sources describes an internal tool for team problem structuring, branching between hypotheses, or a decision log. That suggests whitespace even inside MBB, though internal tools are not fully public.

### Gaps
- I found no published, current (2026) acceptable-use policy text from McKinsey, BCG, Bain or any Big Four firm. Policies are internal. The 2023 items above may be outdated now that enterprise deployments exist.
- Primary BCG figures for Deckster and GENE (beyond second-hand BI citations) were not found. BCG custom-GPT counts conflict.
- EY's primary AI vendor remains unresolved across sources.
- The HBS/BCG study's finding that consultants did worse on tasks outside AI's frontier (often quoted as ~19 percentage points less likely to be correct) is relevant to a "Critic" role but was not verified this session.
- I found nothing on whether firms permit local LLMs (Ollama and similar) on firm laptops. That is a key unknown for Vellum's local-model option.

## 4. What a vendor needs to sell into a consulting firm, and how boutiques buy differently

### Takeaway
Large-firm procurement follows enterprise SaaS norms. Expect a SOC 2 Type II report (US) and/or ISO 27001 (Europe), SAML SSO plus SCIM, a DPA with subprocessor list, data residency (EU), pen-test results, retention and breach terms, and in 2026 an explicit "no training on customer data" commitment and often ISO 42001. Competing consultant-facing tools already advertise these. Consulting firms add client-confidentiality pressure: client contracts can require written approval of AI tools. Boutiques buy like prosumers. They use credit-card team plans ($10–50 per seat per month), approve tools through a short internal policy (enterprise tier or training opt-out), and value senior leverage.

### Cited Findings
**Enterprise security asks (generic; I found no consulting-firm-specific standards)**

- Small-team price points in the adjacent category (Oct 2026): Plus AI Team $30–40/user/mo; Beautiful.ai Team $40–50/seat/mo; Gamma Business $40/member/mo; Claude Team $20–25/seat/mo; ChatGPT Business $20–25/user/mo; Pitch Business €20/mo. — [NOXI](https://noxi.ai/blog/best-ai-presentation-tools-for-consultants); [Oria](https://www.oria.one/resources/best-ai-presentation-tool-strategy-consultants) [vendors]

### Inferences
- For large firms, a cloud Vellum would need the full set: SOC 2 Type II, ISO 27001 (likely ISO 42001 soon), SSO and SCIM, a DPA with a no-training clause, EU residency. That is 6–12 months of compliance work before the first enterprise deal. A local-first desktop build shrinks the data-processing review a lot, because no client data goes to Vellum. But firms will still review the desktop app itself: code signing, update mechanism, telemetry (off or controllable), SBOM, admin and MDM deployment, and which model endpoint the AI calls. Offering "bring your firm's approved model" (Azure OpenAI, firm Claude, local model) avoids adding a new AI subprocessor. Shareable artifacts need an export path that stays inside the firm's SharePoint or Teams.
- Boutiques and independents are the realistic first market. They decide quickly (partner-level), pay by card at $20–50 per seat per month, need client-confidentiality comfort (local-first plus training opt-out fits their policies), and value leverage for senior people over junior staff. Their pain matches Vellum's pitch: senior consultants doing their own synthesis, with no ghost-deck juniors.
- Client confidentiality cuts both ways. Boards that live on the consultant's laptop are attractive, but teams of 3–8 need to share and co-edit. A sync story through firm-controlled storage (SharePoint, OneDrive, Git or a shared drive) would avoid becoming a cloud processor.

### Gaps
- I found no public vendor-onboarding or third-party-risk requirements for McKinsey, BCG, Bain, the Big Four or Accenture.
- I did not research auditor-independence rules. Big Four firms face limits on business relationships with audit clients, which could affect a vendor that is also an audit client.
- I found no quantitative survey of boutique consulting tool spend or buying process. The evidence is anecdotal or comes from a single policy.

### Inferences
- Market whitespace: research tools collect evidence (AlphaSense, Hebbia ≈ Scout), slide tools produce output (≈ Wright), and generic chat gives critique on request (≈ Critic). But no product joins them in a persistent reasoning structure with lettered alternatives, falsifiable tests, evidence weight and a record of rejected options. Vellum's decision trail and branching are its defensible features. They should lead the pitch, ahead of deck generation, which is crowded and cheap ($10–40 per seat per month).
- Positioning versus slide tools: "the map behind the deck". Wright's output should export to firm-template PowerPoint (or hand off to think-cell or Copilot), not compete with them.
- Pricing anchors: individual and boutique use at $20–50 per seat per month sits with Claude Team, ChatGPT Business and Plus AI. A premium team tier ($75–150 per seat per month) is plausible only with collaboration, firm templates and audit-trail export. Research platforms at $10k+ per seat per year show firms pay heavily for evidence quality. Scout connecting to AlphaSense, Tegus or the firm's knowledge base (Lilli, Sage, Verra-like systems) would add value in larger firms.
- Risk: the frontier vendors already deployed firm-wide (Claude Cowork and Managed Agents at KPMG, ChatGPT Enterprise, Copilot Agent Mode) could add canvas or "thinking" features inside the approved tenancy. Vellum's defence is the specific method (hypotheses, tests, trail) and local-first ownership.
