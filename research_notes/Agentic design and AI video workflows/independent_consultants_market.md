# Independent strategy and management consultants as a market for a paid thinking tool (Vellum), as of 2026-11-26

Scope note: the segment covers solo strategy and management consultants (including ex-MBB and Big Four alumni), fractional executives and freelance strategy consultants in the US, UK, Europe and other English-speaking markets. Every claim has a date and a source-quality tag:
- [Primary survey]: a published methodology, from a vendor or a neutral party.
- [Vendor self-report]: a company's own claim about its network or data.
- [Secondary]: an aggregator or blog repeating a figure.
- [Opinion]: commentary or practitioner advice.

Research ran 2026-11-26. Reddit threads could not be retrieved through search, so no r/consulting or r/freelance quotes appear below.

## 1. How large and fast-growing is the independent / freelance consulting segment (US, UK, Europe), and how many ex-MBB / Big Four alumni work independently?

### Takeaway
The broad US pool is very large and growing slowly but steadily. In 2026 there are 74.9M US independents and about 11.5M independent professional service providers (2025), and that group grew 55% from 2020 to 2025. The real target is solo strategy consultants with top-firm training, and that group is far smaller. Curated networks count it in the thousands to low tens of thousands, and nobody publishes a reliable count of ex-MBB or Big Four alumni working independently. UK data suggests independents take a meaningful share of consulting spend, but the best figure (about one fifth, roughly £2bn) dates from 2018.

### Cited Findings
**US: MBO Partners State of Independence (the most rigorous recurring US source; Emergent Research fields it for MBO, a staffing and compliance vendor)**
- 2026 (16th study, fielded April 2026, released 2026-09-28; n=4,557 US adults including 2,063 independents; Emergent Research and Radius Insights; weighted to US demographics) [Primary survey]. Findings:
  - A record 74.9M US independents.
  - Full-time independents have grown 114% since 2020 and are now 22% of all full-time US workers, nearly double the 2019 share.
  - An estimated 5.8M independents earn $100K+, nearly double 2020.
  - One third serve customers outside the US.
  - 78% plan to stay independent or build a larger business.
  - Source: [PR Newswire / MBO 2026 release](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/mbo-partners-by-beeline-us-independent-workforce-reaches-record-74-9-million-as-full-time-independence-accelerates-302890957.html).

  - 72.9M independents, up from 72.7M in 2024.
  - By type: 27.6M full-time (flat), 7.9M part-time (down 29% from 11.1M in 2022) and 37.4M occasional.
  - Full-time independents rose from 13.6M (2020) to 17M (2021), 21.6M (2022), 26M (2023), 27.7M (2024) and 27.6M (2025).
  - Source: [MBO 2025 report PDF](https://info.mbopartners.com/rs/mbo/images/MBO_2025_State_of_Independence_Research_Report.pdf); [MBO 2025 press release](https://www.mbopartners.com/blog/press/2025-state-of-independence-reveals-growing-talent-strategy-for-businesses).
- "Independent Service Professionals" (skilled people who provide services to other businesses, including consultants, SOW contractors and freelancers) grew 3% to 11.5M in 2025. That is the fifth straight year of growth and +55% since 2020. 53% say they have "a lot of choice" over who they work with and 39% "some choice". Source: [MBO 2025 report PDF](https://info.mbopartners.com/rs/mbo/images/MBO_2025_State_of_Independence_Research_Report.pdf), 2025 [Primary survey].

  - Freelancers doing AI work earn 34% more per hour.
  - Caution: the two samples differ ("skilled knowledge workers" n=3,000 against "skilled workers" n=2,400), so the 10-point jump may partly reflect a method change.
  - Source: [Upwork IR release](https://investors.upwork.com/news-releases/news-release-details/upworks-future-workforce-index-2026-how-ai-redefining-value-work); [Upwork research page](https://www.upwork.com/research/research-future-workforce-index-2026).

### Inferences
- The 11.5M US "independent service professionals" figure is far too broad to be Vellum's addressable market. Most of those people are not hypothesis-driven strategy consultants.
- Curated networks give a better order of magnitude for the core persona (solo strategy consultants with top-firm training): Umbrex 8.5k, Graphite 12k, Eden McCallum 500+, and Catalant's claimed 100k. Members overlap heavily across networks. A defensible guess is tens of thousands of top-tier-trained independents across the US, UK and Europe, with a much larger second ring of hundreds of thousands of general independent and fractional consultants (UK about 256k "independents" if the ONS-derived figure holds; US fractional figures of 100k+). This is an inference, not a measured figure.
- Growth is strong among high earners (5.8M at $100K+, nearly double 2020) and full-time independents (+114% since 2020). These are the people most likely to pay for professional tools. Growth in total headcount is flat.
- The UK has the best evidence that clients buy independents directly, but the 2018 data needs refreshing.

### Gaps
- I found no credible count of ex-MBB or Big Four alumni working as independents. Firms publish total alumni at most, and Big Four alumni counts were not found at all.
- I found no primary Source Global Research or Kennedy/ALM report on the independent consulting segment from 2024 to 2026. The best Source figure, about 1/5 of UK spend, is from 2018.
- Continental European counts of independent management consultants (Germany, France, Netherlands, Nordics) were not found. Malt Strategy (formerly Comatch) network size was not found.
- The ONS-derived 256,200 UK "independents" figure has an unclear definition and should be verified against ONS self-employment by occupation (management consultants and business analysts) before use.
- The HBR pieces on independent professionals that were suggested as sources were not retrieved.

## 2. What do independent consultants earn and spend on software; which tools do they use for thinking, structuring and deliverables, and what do they pay?

### Takeaway
Top-tier independents bill high day rates; Umbrex's rule of thumb gives about $2,000 a day for someone who would earn $250k employed, at 50–70% utilisation. Each tool they use already costs roughly $100–$1,200 a year. ChatGPT is the universal AI anchor, and PowerPoint plus think-cell remains the delivery standard. No credible survey measures what independent consultants as a group spend on software.

### Cited Findings
- Earnings:
  - 5.6M US independents earned $100K+ in 2025 and about 5.8M in 2026. Source: [MBO 2025 PDF](https://info.mbopartners.com/rs/mbo/images/MBO_2025_State_of_Independence_Research_Report.pdf); [MBO 2026 release](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/mbo-partners-by-beeline-us-independent-workforce-reaches-record-74-9-million-as-full-time-independence-accelerates-302890957.html) [Primary survey].
  - In the UK, about 70% of independents say their pay is fairer than when employed. Independents rate financial security 1.5 on a 3-point scale, against 2.1 for employed consultants. Source: [Consultancy.uk, Eden McCallum/LBS/HPA, 2025-03-04](https://www.consultancy.uk/news/39583/independents-consultants-satisfied-with-their-work-life-and-career-freedom) [Primary survey].
- Day-rate heuristic: double the full-time salary you would expect and divide by 250, so a $250,000 salary gives about $2,000 a day. Typical independent utilisation is "50, 60, 70%". Source: [Umbrex, pricing your services](https://umbrex.com/become-a-consultant/pricing-your-services/); [Umbrex Unleashed ep. 174](https://umbrex.com/unleashed/episode-174-how-to-set-consulting-fees/) [Opinion, from a credible practitioner network].
- In a Catalant community survey, consultants who could quantify AI savings mostly estimated more than $5,000 saved in 2024, and some saved "entire days per week". Source: [Catalant, Jay Dwivedi, 2025-07-31](https://catalant.com/digital-and-ai/how-genai-is-reshaping-the-industry-and-empowering-independent-consultants/) [Small, self-selected survey; anecdotal].
- Tools in use:
  - Eden McCallum/LBS 2024 survey of 500 UK and European consultants: ChatGPT is the most common AI tool, about half use Microsoft Copilot occasionally, and 30% use Gemini in some situations. Source: [Consultancy.uk, 2025-03-26](https://www.consultancy.uk/news/39763/just-one-in-ten-consultants-always-using-ai-in-their-work) [Primary survey].
  - Catalant independents: ChatGPT is the "universal anchor tool". Peers use more than 25 GenAI tools, including ChatGPT Team, custom GPTs and Ollama (local models for privacy). Tasks: research, sales, data analysis, first drafts of proposals, decks and emails, document parsing, meeting summaries, due diligence, market synthesis and scenario planning. "Digital sprawl" (too many tools) is named as a pain. Source: [Catalant, 2025-07-31](https://catalant.com/digital-and-ai/how-genai-is-reshaping-the-industry-and-empowering-independent-consultants/) [Anecdotal survey].
  - The CMI's 2026 trends piece for independents names ChatGPT and Claude as the example tools. Source: [CMI, Ian Wylie, 2026-01-27](https://www.managers.org.uk/knowledge-and-insights/article/top-trends-for-independent-consultants-in-2026) [Opinion].
  - A practitioner workflow: draft the storyline in a general chatbot, build visuals in Gamma or Pitch, then export to PowerPoint or Google Slides for final editing. The author warns of outdated or made-up statistics. Source: [Business Meets AI Substack](https://businessmeetsai.substack.com/p/from-banal-to-brilliant-make-consulting) [Opinion].
- List prices (per user):
  - think-cell, billed annually in USD: Essentials $8.90/month (about $107/yr), Suite $28.60/month (about $343/yr), AI Suite $46.54/month (about $558/yr), AI Suite Plus $97.60/month (about $1,171/yr). Single-user licences are available and licensing is per user. Source: [think-cell pricing, retrieved 2026-11-26](https://www.thinkcell.com/en/pricing) [Primary, vendor price list].
  - Miro: Free (3 editable boards), Starter $8 per member per month and Business $20 per member per month, billed yearly, with 25 and 50 AI credits per month respectively. Source: [Miro pricing, retrieved 2026-11-26](https://miro.com/pricing/) [Primary, vendor price list].
  - Gamma: from $9 per seat per month (Plus) up to $90 per seat per month (Ultra). A flat fee plus AI credits applies since October 2025. Source: [Orb pricing atlas capture, Oct 2026](https://www.withorb.com/pricing-atlas/gamma); [Gamma pricing](https://gamma.app/pricing) [Secondary capture of a vendor price list].
  - ChatGPT Business: $20 per seat per month billed annually or $25 monthly, with a **minimum of 2 seats**. Premium seats are $100 per month (annual) or $125 (monthly) as of August 2026. OpenAI does not train on Business workspace data. Source: [OpenAI business pricing](https://openai.com/business/chatgpt-pricing/); [OpenAI Help Center, ChatGPT Business overview](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/8792828-chatgpt-business-overview); [OpenAI premium seats](https://openai.com/index/premium-seats-chatgpt-business) [Primary, vendor].
- Freelancer software spend in general:
  - A median of $187 a month comes from Mewayz's own solopreneur users. Source: [Mewayz](https://mewayz.com/tl/blog/the-solopreneur-tech-budget-a-data-driven-breakdown-of-average-monthly-software-spend) [Vendor data].
  - Other figures of $247 a month and $518 a month are attributed to surveys that cannot be traced. Source: [Fastlancer Freelancer Tool Report 2026](https://www.fastlancer.org/en/fastlancer-blog/freelancer-tool-report-2026/); [Clockify](https://clockify.me/how-freelancers-spend-time) [Secondary, unverified].

### Inferences
- These consultants bill around $1,500–$2,500 a day. If a tool saves even a few hours at kickoff and synthesis, $20–$50 a month pays for itself many times over. Willingness to pay depends on trust and fit, not on price.
- The existing anchors per thinking or deck tool are think-cell at $107–$1,171 a year, Miro at $96–$240 a year, Gamma from about $108 a year, and ChatGPT at $240–$300 a year. A Vellum price of roughly $15–$40 a month sits within the per-tool envelope these buyers already accept.
- ChatGPT Business's 2-seat minimum means a solo consultant who wants "no training on my data" terms pays for two seats or relies on consumer opt-outs. That gap is real for one-person firms (see question 3).
- Deliverables still converge on PowerPoint, and think-cell's existence and AI tiers show the deck is the unit of value. Vellum's "Wright builds a deck" step probably needs to export clean PowerPoint, or it will compete with think-cell and Gamma rather than complement them.

### Gaps
- I found no survey of software spend or tool stacks specific to independent management consultants.
- Claude Pro, Notion, FigJam, Mural and PowerPoint/M365 pricing was not retrieved in this pass.
- I found no reliable distribution of day rates for independent strategy consultants in 2025–2026 from a survey, only heuristics. The 2025 Eden McCallum survey summary omits rates and utilisation.

## 3. How have they adopted AI in 2025–2026, what limits it (NDAs, data clauses, client bans, accuracy), and is local / on-device AI a real buying reason?

### Takeaway
Adoption is broad but shallow. About three quarters of US independents use GenAI (2025), but only about 1 in 10 European consultants always use it, and synthesis is the task they least trust it with. The main brakes are accuracy and hallucination (69% concerned) and confidentiality: client contracts increasingly carry clauses banning client data from AI, and consumer AI plans train on data by default. Local AI shows up as a practitioner workaround (Ollama), but the only evidence that it is a buying reason comes from anecdotes and vendor marketing.

### Cited Findings
- MBO 2025 (US independents) [Primary survey]. Source: [MBO 2025 PDF](https://info.mbopartners.com/rs/mbo/images/MBO_2025_State_of_Independence_Research_Report.pdf).
  - 74% use GenAI, up from 65% in 2024, against 69% of traditional workers.
  - 24% use it frequently. About 70% find it highly beneficial (28% extremely useful, 39% very useful).
  - Uses: research 41%, generating new ideas 37%, writing and creative work 35%, data analysis and visualisation 21%.
  - Reported benefits: deeper insights 65%, automating repetitive tasks 64%, productivity 63%, expanding client offerings 62%, complementing their work 61%.
  - Time saved: mean 9 hours a week, median 5.
  - Only 23% are "worried" about AI.
- MBO 2026: nearly 8 in 10 independents say AI complements their work and improves productivity, and GenAI users report nearly 10 hours a week saved. Source: [MBO 2026 release](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/mbo-partners-by-beeline-us-independent-workforce-reaches-record-74-9-million-as-full-time-independence-accelerates-302890957.html) [Primary survey].
- Upwork 2025: 54% of skilled freelancers report advanced or expert AI skills, against 38% of full-time employees. Source: [Upwork FWI 2025](https://www.upwork.com/research/future-workforce-index-2025) [Vendor survey].
- Eden McCallum/LBS "Consultant Survey 2024" (n=500 UK and European consultants, independents and employed mixed; published 2025-03-26) [Primary survey]. Source: [Consultancy.uk](https://www.consultancy.uk/news/39763/just-one-in-ten-consultants-always-using-ai-in-their-work).
  - 79% use AI at least sometimes; only 10% always.
  - Idea generation: 76% use AI, but only 9% every time.
  - **Synthesis (comparing conflicting viewpoints): only 7% always use AI.**
  - Data analysis: 51% use it at least occasionally, 1% always. No one always uses it for project planning or admin.
  - 69% are concerned about hallucinations to some extent. 23% are "very concerned" about source validation.
- Catalant independents (July 2025): every respondent used AI daily. Data security is a key challenge where engagements involve proprietary research, client IP or confidential documents. Peers are "moving to secure environments and local models" (Ollama is named) and call for standardised governance. Source: [Catalant](https://catalant.com/digital-and-ai/how-genai-is-reshaping-the-industry-and-empowering-independent-consultants/) [Anecdotal].
- Contract restrictions [Legal commentary and templates; no prevalence data]:
  - Clauses that expressly bar using client data in AI without consent are spreading. Use-limitation and data-destruction clauses written before GenAI may also reach GenAI use. Many were drafted in 2023–2024 out of fear of training on client data. Source: [NYU Compliance & Enforcement blog, 2025-12-01](https://wp.nyu.edu/compliance_enforcement/2025/12/01/ais-biggest-enterprise-challenge-in-2026-contractual-use-limitations-on-data/).
  - Thomson Reuters Practical Law has a standard US B2B clause that bars using customer data as AI input or prompts without prior authorisation. Source: [Practical Law](https://uk.practicallaw.thomsonreuters.com/w-042-6410?transitionType=Default&contextData=%28sc.Default%29&firstPage=true).
  - The common compromise bans public or consumer GenAI tools but allows controlled enterprise systems, provided confidential data is not shared externally or used for training or fine-tuning. Source: [Carta, AI clauses in NDAs](https://carta.com/blog/ai-clauses-nda/).
  - Commentary distinguishes a "tool ban" (no GenAI in performing the services) from a "data ban" (no client confidential information in any AI system). The data ban is said to be more common in regulated sectors. Source: [Versely](https://www.versely.studio/blog/when-a-client-contract-forbids-ai-tools); [Layerz](https://layerz.cc/blog/client-contract-prohibits-ai-financial-data) [Opinion/blog].
  - Subconsultant flowdown clauses impose the prime contractor's AI terms on subcontractors. This matters for independents staffed through networks or Big Four talent pools. Source: [Dan Cumberland Labs, January 2026](https://dancumberlandlabs.com/blog/contract-engineering-services-inc/) [Blog].
- Consumer AI terms:
  - Anthropic's 2025-08-28 update made model training on Claude Free, Pro and Max chats the default, with an opt-out. Data from users who stay opted in is kept for up to 5 years, against 30 days for those who opt out. Commercial plans (Claude for Work, API) are excluded. Source: [Anthropic](https://anthropic.com/news/updates-to-our-consumer-terms); [TechCrunch, 2025-08-28](https://www.techcrunch.com/2025/08/28/anthropic-users-face-a-new-choice-opt-out-or-share-your-data-for-ai-training/) [Primary].
  - OpenAI does not train on ChatGPT Business data, but Business needs at least 2 seats. Source: [OpenAI Help Center](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/8792828-chatgpt-business-overview) [Primary].
- Local AI claims [Vendor marketing]:
  - Vendor guides argue that "data never leaves the device" is a competitive advantage for solo consultants who compete on trust. Source: [Elephas](https://elephas.app/resources/how-consultants-use-ai-without-exposing-client-data).
  - A May 2026 professional-services guide describes hybrid models, with cloud AI for general work and local models for confidential work, as increasingly common. Source: [SysGenPro](https://sysgenpro.com/ai/professional-services-decision-guide-to-local-llm-deployment-for-data-privacy).
  - I found no independent figure for how many consultants run local models.
- The CMI (UK) says AI is now an "assumed capability" for independents, who must show "responsible, transparent use". Clients want faster delivery, measurable outcomes and less reliance on long reports. Source: [CMI, 2026-01-27](https://www.managers.org.uk/knowledge-and-insights/article/top-trends-for-independent-consultants-in-2026) [Opinion].
- A widely circulated statistic that "45% of consulting firms have banned public ChatGPT for client data" has **no traceable primary source**. Do not use it. Source: [Gitnux](https://gitnux.org/ai-in-the-management-consulting-industry-statistics/) [Unreliable aggregator].

### Inferences
- Independents use AI heavily for drafting and research but rarely trust it for synthesis or judgment: 7% always use it for synthesis, and hallucination concern is high. Vellum's design addresses that distrust directly. Evidence is labelled Observed, Reported, Proposal or Illustration, and the Critic role and the human-decides model fit it. The positioning should stress traceable synthesis rather than more generation.
- Solo consultants face a structural confidentiality gap. Consumer plans train by default unless the user opts out, enterprise plans with no-training terms are sized for teams (ChatGPT Business needs 2 seats), and client data-ban clauses may prohibit any third-party AI. A local-first board with an optional local model is a credible answer and a clean story to tell clients ("your data never leaves my machine"). Whether it is a reason to buy rather than a reason not to reject is unproven. Interviews should test it, for example with "Has a client contract stopped you using AI on their data?" and "Would you pay more for local-only?"
- Data bans in regulated sectors and Big Four subcontractor flowdowns suggest the confidentiality pain is sharpest for independents serving financial services, healthcare, PE due diligence and public sector clients. Those are candidate beachhead verticals.

### Gaps
- I found no survey measuring how often clients ban AI or restrict data use for independent consultants specifically.
- I found no survey breaking out AI adoption for independents against firm-employed strategy consultants. The Eden McCallum survey mixes both.
- I found no independent evidence that local or on-device AI is a purchase driver for consultants. Evidence is limited to the Catalant anecdote (Ollama) and vendor marketing.

## 4. Where do they gather and how are they reached?

### Takeaway
Online channels now lead. 42% of US independents find work mainly through online tools, against 14% in 2015, and roughly half to two thirds use talent platforms, averaging 3–4 each. For top-tier strategy independents, the high-signal channels are curated networks (Umbrex, Catalant, Graphite/Toptal, BTG, Eden McCallum, Malt Strategy, Movemeon), firm alumni networks (McKinsey's 70k), LinkedIn, and a small set of podcasts and newsletters (Unleashed, Consulting Success). Word of mouth is still large but shrinking.

## 5. What are their biggest workflow pains at kickoff and synthesis?

### Takeaway
Direct evidence on kickoff and synthesis pain for solo consultants is thin. The best-documented pains are commercial: selling, the first projects, pipeline and financial insecurity. Indirect evidence points to three workflow pains that match Vellum's thesis: low trust in AI for synthesis (7% always use it), fear of hallucinated or unsourced facts in client deliverables, and pressure from clients for faster, more practical, co-created outputs instead of long reports. Isolation (no team to whiteboard with) is widely mentioned but not quantified for strategy consultants.

### Cited Findings
- The two most-cited hardest parts of going independent are selling one's services and landing the first three projects. Source: [Career Pivot Substack](https://mypivot.substack.com/i/160744967/marketing-and-administration-hidden-costs); [Data Analysis Journal, "0 to $685k"](https://dataanalysis.substack.com/p/my-consulting-journey-0-to-685k) [Opinion/first-person].
- Financial security: UK independents rate it 1.5 on a 3-point scale, against 2.1 for employed consultants. Over 90% say they have more control, and 89% are satisfied. Source: [Consultancy.uk, 2025-03-04](https://www.consultancy.uk/news/39583/independents-consultants-satisfied-with-their-work-life-and-career-freedom) [Primary survey].
- Isolation is listed among the key challenges of independent consulting, with advice to seek mentors or peers (Umbrex FAQ; the page body could not be retrieved, so this rests on the search excerpt). Professional isolation is also a named pain point for independent consultants in adjacent fields. Source: [Umbrex FAQ](https://umbrex.com/resources/independent-consulting-101/what-are-the-key-challenges-of-being-an-independent-consultant/); [Mark Sklarow Substack](https://marksklarow.substack.com/p/the-hidden-frictions-in-educational) [Opinion].
- Academic work on independent management consultants describes peer communities as high-trust groups that have become alternatives to formal associations, through networks, platforms and communities of practice. Source: [Management Consulting Journal, 2024](https://reference-global.com/2/v2/download/article/10.2478/mcj-2024-0012.pdf) [Academic].
- Synthesis and AI trust: only 7% always use AI for synthesis (comparing conflicting viewpoints), and 9% every time for idea generation. 69% are concerned about hallucinations and 23% are very concerned about source validation. Source: [Consultancy.uk, Eden McCallum/LBS, 2025-03-26](https://www.consultancy.uk/news/39763/just-one-in-ten-consultants-always-using-ai-in-their-work) [Primary survey].
- Deliverable pressure: clients are "far more focused on practical impact" than on frameworks or long reports. Long engagements and extended discovery phases are becoming unattractive. Shorter cycles, live problem-solving and co-creation with client teams are replacing end-of-project delivery. Consultants who rely on traditional presentations "will find it harder to be valued", and AI is expected to take over much research, synthesis and content development. Source: [CMI, 2026-01-27](https://www.managers.org.uk/knowledge-and-insights/article/top-trends-for-independent-consultants-in-2026) [Opinion, from practising independents].
- Slides workflow: practitioners draft the storyline in a chatbot, build visuals in Gamma or Pitch, then export to PowerPoint. AI tools may produce outdated or made-up statistics. Source: [Business Meets AI Substack](https://businessmeetsai.substack.com/p/from-banal-to-brilliant-make-consulting) [Opinion].
- Tool sprawl and uneven results: independents report fragmentation from too many AI tools, and results vary with prompt-engineering skill. Source: [Catalant, 2025-07-31](https://catalant.com/digital-and-ai/how-genai-is-reshaping-the-industry-and-empowering-independent-consultants/) [Anecdotal].
- Solo consultants build "AI research assistant" setups (for example a Claude Project fed with client briefs, competitor sites and industry reports) to compress research. The author reports about 5 hours saved per client. Source: [GenAI Unplugged Substack](https://genaiunplugged.substack.com/p/ai-research-assistant-claude-projects) [Opinion, self-reported].

### Inferences
- A tool's message works best if it leads with outcomes buyers already feel: "an answer on day one, with a defensible storyline by week one", "every claim traceable to a source", "a sparring partner when you have no team". Abstract "decision maps" are a harder sell. The Strategist's lettered directions, each with a test it could fail, and the Critic role substitute for the missing team whiteboard session. That claim is plausible but has not been validated directly.
- Clients' shift toward co-creation and shorter cycles favours a living, shareable decision board and trail over a final long deck. The deck still matters as the artifact clients pay for, so the Wright role's PowerPoint export quality is likely make-or-break.
- Commercial pains (selling, pipeline) dominate what independents say. A thinking tool that also makes proposals and day-one points of view faster, using the same issue tree for the pitch deck, could tie the product to the pain they rank highest.

### Gaps
- I found no survey quantifying kickoff, structuring or synthesis pain, or hours spent building slides, for solo strategy consultants.
- Reddit and community voice-of-customer on "structuring alone" and "turning interviews into a storyline" was not retrievable. Primary interviews (for example 10–15 Umbrex or Catalant members) are needed.
- I found no data on how often independents subcontract slide production (to freelance designers or offshore deck services), which would indicate both willingness to pay and the size of the deck pain.
